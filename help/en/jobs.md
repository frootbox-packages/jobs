Under **Jobs** you keep your open positions, apprenticeships and
internships. Each job has a title, a short teaser for the list and search
engines and a text with the tasks, the profile and what you offer.

## Create a job

Choose **New job** and give it a title as people search for it, such as
"Barista (m/f/d)". If you like, add:

- the **kind of job**, e.g. full time and part time, several are fine,
- the **start**, empty means as soon as possible,
- the **workplace**: on site, partly remote or remote,
- the **salary** from and up to, per hour, month or year,
- the **location** and the **contact** from **People**,
- an **image**.

The job stays a draft until you choose **Publish**. Under **Times** you
set when it goes online and when it goes offline by itself, e.g. at the
end of applications.

## Show it on the website

A page shows the jobs when an admin chooses it in its settings under
**This page shows**. The open jobs appear there, the newest first. When the jobs have different categories, kinds or locations, visitors can
filter by them. Each job gets its own address below that page, e.g.
`/jobs/barista-m-f-d`.

Choose an **application form** from **Forms** in the settings of the
list in the editing mode. It appears on every job, and each entry names under **Regarding**
which job it is about. With a field **Files** applicants send their CV
and references. Without a form, a job offers to apply by email to the
contact.

For apprenticeships with a page of their own, e.g.
`/careers/apprenticeships`, an admin adds another **space** there.

The block **Newest jobs** shows the newest jobs, e.g. on the start page,
from all spaces or from one, with a link to all of them.

## Categories

Under **Categories** you create areas such as "Kitchen" or "Service",
also below each other. Visitors can filter the list by them. Deleting a
category keeps its jobs.

## Tags

Tick the tags that fit or type new words, separated by commas, e.g.
"Career change" or "Early shift". When the jobs have different tags,
visitors can filter the list by them. A teaser of the newest jobs can
show only those with one tag.

## Search engines

Each job brings structured data so search engines know it as a job
posting: kind, location, start, salary and until when one can apply.
